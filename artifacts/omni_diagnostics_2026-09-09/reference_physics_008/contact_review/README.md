# Actual008: RR unload was mistaken for a qualified flight

The run completed seven measured landings, including all six legs, then rejected RR's second swing at 20.74 s. All 32 retrieved raw payload hashes match the terminal audit. This report preserves the failed run; it does not qualify a successor.

RR's new swing began at 20.42 s. The two samples at 20.70 and 20.72 s have exactly zero reported normal force; the return at 20.74 s has 8.892 N. Contact points remain finite. Toe height is only 23.9, 29.2 and 3.9 micrometres, respectively. The recorded rise from the last loaded sample is 8.407 micrometres, far below the unchanged 2 mm flight requirement. This occurs at 16% of the planned swing, before its apex or any qualified descent.

The virtual toe is also still at −0.0504 mm: its 1.0876 mm lift has only just offset the loaded anchor at −1.1380 mm. The current state machine latches `flight_seen` from two force-free samples alone, then treats any returned contact as a landing attempt. The evidence supports a short unloading/recontact episode rather than a completed airborne step. This is not merely force hovering around the 1 N classification threshold: the recorded forces are zero, zero and 8.892 N. The finite contact points alone do not establish load-bearing support.

The previous seven continuous force-free runs last 44–64 samples and attain 3.24–5.26 mm measured lift. They remain meaningful progress. The failed RR stride is 70.30 mm; its first stride was 46.30 mm and reached 5.05 mm lift. At rejection, current virtual planar travel is 4.072 mm and measured toe travel from swing start is 1.608 mm. Most of the measured travel occurs during the two force-free samples, while vertical clearance remains negligible. That is a reason to keep the clearance requirement and future obstacle checks, not to claim the foot has safely cleared terrain.

The post-settle trace retains at least five distal supports and peaks at 1.2542 N·m requested torque. No new torque, collision, support, timing, speed or landing bound should be relaxed to address this classification problem. Reported toe velocity disagrees strongly with position differences during the force-free samples; the separate planned joint-angle observer must investigate that solver/reporting limitation. This review uses measured positions to establish the tiny clearance and does not replace any existing physical velocity gate.

The narrow proposed successor adds an explicit unloading state. Two consecutive force-free samples and the existing 2 mm measured rise are both required before latching qualified flight. A contact return before qualification resets the consecutive count and baseline; raw samples, runs and rebounds remain recorded. The existing planned apex supplies a finite qualification deadline without a new tuning parameter. Once qualified, every prior early-contact, apex/descent, landing-speed, 12 mm correction, contact-loss, sustained-support and stop check remains active. Sustained failure to lift must still reject. This proposal must be versioned, replayed against historical failures and independently reviewed before another physical trial.
