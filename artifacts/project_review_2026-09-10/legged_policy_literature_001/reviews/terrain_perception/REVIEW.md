# Terrain, perception, and navigation: breadth-first evidence review

Prepared 2026-09-10. Twelve representative locomotion/mapping works plus one explicitly tentative cross-domain match for the user's earlier “super drone paper.” This is a primary-source research comparison, not a reproduced training result, a hardware qualification, or a change to the current controller. [sources.json](sources.json) records reading scope, links, and project-evidence hashes.

The strongest next approach is **parallel proprioceptive terrain control, an ideal-terrain teacher, and sensor acquisition/replay**. Do not wait for a perfected blind terrain policy before working on perception. Equally, do not make a newly integrated depth camera the only route to balance. Miki's perceptive controller provides the closest teacher/student pattern; Lee and RMA motivate a robust proprioceptive component; CMS is a serious alternative when realistic visual simulation is the bottleneck. These are transferable design principles, not evidence that any existing quadruped checkpoint or reward scales to this 8.2608 kg, 1.6 N·m hexapod. [Miki 2022](https://leggedrobotics.github.io/rl-perceptiveloco/), [Lee 2020](https://leggedrobotics.github.io/rl-blindloco/), [RMA](https://ashish-kmr.github.io/rma-legged-robots/), [CMS](https://antonilo.github.io/vision_locomotion/).

## Representative approaches

The “use here” and “limit” columns are engineering inferences. The cited papers establish the published method, not the proposed hexapod outcome. No repository was installed or executed during this review.

| Work and primary source | Method and deployment inputs | Use here; decisive limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **Lee et al., 2020 — Learning Quadrupedal Locomotion over Challenging Terrain**. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11251), [author project](https://leggedrobotics.github.io/rl-blindloco/). | Sim-trained omnidirectional control from proprioceptive history, demonstrated on varied natural ground. It does not require a terrain camera for its locomotion feedback. | Establish a blind, mild-terrain comparison and behavior during sensor loss. Proprioception responds after interaction; it cannot reveal an unseen gap before a foot commits. Do not interpret successful rubble/snow demonstrations as permission to treat unknown cells as support. |
| **Kumar et al., 2021 — RMA: Rapid Motor Adaptation for Legged Robots**. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04034), [author project](https://ashish-kmr.github.io/rma-legged-robots/). | A base policy and adaptation module learn in simulation; recent experience estimates changing environmental/hardware conditions. | History-based adaptation is relevant to payload, traction, and actuator mismatch. It requires informative and trustworthy proprioception; our raw SDK rates and interval-angle evidence must remain distinct. Adaptation neither supplies terrain look-ahead nor creates missing torque headroom. |
| **Miki et al., 2022 — Learning robust perceptive locomotion for quadrupedal robots in the wild**. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08117), [author project](https://leggedrobotics.github.io/rl-perceptiveloco/). | Privileged simulation teacher; student learns teacher actions and terrain reconstruction from noisy observations. Recurrent belief/attention combines proprioception with samples from a robot-centric elevation map. | Best primary template for privileged terrain → imperfect sensing while retaining proprioceptive feedback. The robot still needs a calibrated, timed map pipeline. A learned belief may be useful for control without turning its inferred terrain into independently observed support. |
| **Loquercio et al., 2023 — Learning Visual Locomotion with Cross-Modal Supervision (CMS)**. [Author paper/project](https://antonilo.github.io/vision_locomotion/), [official code](https://github.com/antonilo/rl_locomotion). | Start with a sim-trained blind policy. Monocular RGB learning uses time-shifted real proprioception as supervision for upcoming terrain. | Credible alternative to photorealistic visual simulation or a fragile explicit map. It needs a robot capable of collecting useful real walking data. The reported short data collection is not a schedule estimate for our hardware, and exploring unsupported terrain to generate labels is not justified. |
| **Cheng et al., 2024 — Extreme Parkour with Legged Robots**. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14341), [project](https://extreme-parkour.github.io/), [official code](https://github.com/chengxuxin/extreme-parkour). | Privileged geometric training distilled to front depth sensing, with obstacle-conditioned motion and heading. | Useful for depth noise, terrain curricula, and skill learning after basic control works. Its obstacle progression/heading objective is not equivalent to independently tracking every translation/yaw command. Dynamic jumping capability and the old Isaac Gym software stack do not transfer directly. |
| **Hoeller et al., 2024 — ANYmal Parkour: Learning Agile Navigation for Quadrupedal Robots**. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14874), [author project](https://sites.google.com/leggedrobotics.com/agile-navigation). | Learned 3D reconstruction, several locomotion skills, and a high-level selector. Low-level commands specify local position, heading, and time, allowing motion modulation. | Strong later alternative when obstacles require climbing/crouching rather than continuous walking. Requires body-clearance reasoning beyond a single surface height. Learned completion of occluded geometry is not observed evidence; contacts with other body parts in these tasks differ from our current forbidden-contact gates. |
| **Fu et al., 2022 — Coupling Vision and Proprioception for Navigation of Legged Robots (VP-Nav)**. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02094), [author project](https://navigation-locomotion.github.io/). | Onboard vision builds occupancy/cost maps; a planner and command generator feed the walking policy. Proprioceptive feedback adjusts perceived obstacles and feasible speed. | Closest fit to planner → body-twist control with feedback about actual locomotion capability. It motivates reporting admitted commands, tracking errors, and stop capability upstream. Its recovery after contacting visually missed obstacles is not an acceptable substitute for our collision gate. |
| **He et al., 2024 — Agile But Safe (ABS)**. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17583), [project](https://agile-but-safe.github.io/), [official code](https://github.com/LeCAR-Lab/ABS). | Agile policy, learned reach-avoid value, recovery policy, and depth-to-ray representation. Recovery chooses 2D translation/yaw commands. | A useful candidate for action-conditioned supervision rather than merely penalizing collisions. The learned value and fallback must be validated for our dynamics, occlusion, and negative obstacles. It does not certify an untested hexapod stopping trajectory. |
| **Fankhauser et al., 2018 — Probabilistic Terrain Mapping for Mobile Robots with Uncertain Localization**. [Paper DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/LRA.2018.2849506), [official implementation](https://github.com/ANYbotics/elevation_mapping). | Robot-centric elevation mapping propagates sensor and pose uncertainty; local representation accounts for accumulated localization drift. | Good basis for height/variance/observed layers and registration-aware footprint queries. The referenced repository is no longer actively maintained and uses ROS 1. Retaining an estimate is different from keeping it valid indefinitely; inpainted terrain must remain distinguishable from measurements. |
| **Miki et al., 2022 — Elevation Mapping for Locomotion and Navigation using GPU**. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12876), [official code](https://github.com/leggedrobotics/elevation_mapping_cupy). | GPU elevation processing with additional navigation/locomotion features; demonstrated with legged systems. | Practical implementation candidate after the CPU map contract is proven. Benchmark acquisition-to-command latency and memory on the eventual onboard computer. Spark training throughput does not establish deployable mapping latency; no package installation here demonstrates ROS 2 integration. |
| **Xu et al., 2022 — FAST-LIO2** (2021 preprint). [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.06829), [official code](https://github.com/hku-mars/FAST_LIO). | LiDAR–IMU odometry, direct raw-point scan-to-map registration, and an incremental spatial map. | Sensible first local-pose baseline for the Mid-360 route. Requires the actual driver message, per-point timing, LiDAR–IMU calibration, and moving-scan deskew to be checked. Local odometry without RTK is possible; bounded global position over a mission is not established by that fact. |
| **Zheng et al., 2024 preprint — FAST-LIVO2**. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14035), [official code](https://github.com/hku-mars/FAST-LIVO2). | Tightly coupled LiDAR, IMU, and image updates in a shared geometric representation. Authors also provide synchronized equipment and calibration resources. | An alternative if LiDAR-only geometry becomes poorly constrained. It adds camera calibration, synchronization, image quality, and compute requirements. It does not mean the owned D455/Mid-360 combination is already synchronized or qualified; compare it against the simpler LiDAR–IMU baseline first. |

## What this changes about the plan

**Keep the terrain teacher and sensing work independent enough to diagnose failures.** Run the same modest height/roughness cases with: (A) proprioception only, (B) ideal geometry, and (C) causal noisy/degraded sensor maps. If B fails, mounting another camera is unlikely to fix the controller. If B passes and C fails, examine coverage, pose registration, age, and student inference separately. If A works as well as B on a chosen mild course, retain that simpler behavior there, but test gaps and precise footholds separately. This is a proposed experimental design inspired by the complementary methods above; it is not a published claim that any one architecture wins on our robot.

**Do not add terrain channels to an unchanged actor and call it perceptive.** The current reference state includes contact qualification, landing, command settling, and target/residual history. A terrain student needs an explicitly new observation/checkpoint lineage, terrain noise/dropout training, and comparisons against its own teacher. A recurrent encoder is a candidate, not an automatic solution. It can preserve information and learn disagreement between sensors; it can also preserve the wrong terrain estimate.

**Body twist remains appropriate for the current continuous-walking milestone.** Express forward/left/yaw requests together, including arcs, and expose requested, admitted, filtered, and actual motion separately. Let a future planner limit speed using demonstrated capability rather than an arbitrary radius formula. Later obstacle skills may instead need position/heading/time goals: ANYmal-Parkour is concrete evidence for that alternative. Keeping a twist-based walking interface does not require every future maneuver to use it. [VP-Nav](https://navigation-locomotion.github.io/), [ANYmal-Parkour](https://sites.google.com/leggedrobotics.com/agile-navigation).

**A height map is a useful first representation, not a complete world model.** It describes ground for ordinary stepping; overhangs and body clearance need a separate occupancy/3D channel. A geometrically visible surface may be a pit bottom, thin object, vegetation, or uncertain material. Keep observation validity, geometry, and eligibility to bear load separate. Learned reconstruction may guide exploration while remaining marked inferred. [Elevation mapping](https://github.com/ANYbotics/elevation_mapping), [ANYmal-Parkour](https://sites.google.com/leggedrobotics.com/agile-navigation).

## The actual sensing and stopping conflict

The frozen local replay already distinguishes instantaneous visibility, retained usable cells, and whole proposed-footprint coverage. In its synthetic flat scene with a hypothetical six-D405 rig, **169/377 queries had the entire footprint fresh now; 0/377 had evidence valid through the original planned use time**. This was a causal replay of actual004 motion, not a real-camera trial or terrain traversal. Its 40 ms receipt delay leaves at most 210 ms of a 250 ms capture-age lease. The admitted009 reference took **5.54 s** from stop request to reference quiet, followed by 2 s excluded settling; its final quiet window lasted 12.46 s. [Replay and limitations](../../sequential_footprint_reacquisition_001/README.md), [actual009 state](../../reference_physics_results_009/run/wave/state.json).

No reviewed paper supplies a justification for silently stretching that lease to cover a multi-second swing or stop. Conversely, a blanket 250 ms expiry is a prototype rule, not a law of terrain: persistent static geometry could remain informative if pose uncertainty, scene change, and occlusion are modeled and tested. **Keep the current rule unchanged in this work, but test that assumption instead of treating it as settled.** A revised rule would need separate evidence and versioning.

The next useful experiment measures whether each new footprint can be reacquired before commitment and whether the physically executed stopping/support envelope remains supported after acquisition loss. Periodic future frames are not observations. A planted measured contact can support the current pose without certifying another cell. If the available controller cannot stop before entering an unknown region, an evidence checker alone cannot make that transition safe. Needed options are a demonstrated shorter stop/hold, a longer already-observed admissible corridor, or a separately tested conservative transition. None exists merely because a software flag says “abort.” ABS motivates studying a learned recovery supervisor; it does not remove this physical requirement. [ABS](https://agile-but-safe.github.io/).

## Owned sensors and missing measurements

The available Mid-360 and D455 are useful starting instruments, not restrictions on eventual purchases. The **D455 minimum optical depth is 520 mm at 1280×720, 350 mm at 848×480, and 260 mm at 640×360**. Near-foot visibility must use the selected calibrated profile, stereo overlap, and depth validity; nominal FOV alone overcounts coverage. These are minimum-depth specifications, not guarantees of useful outdoor depth at every pixel. [D400 datasheet, revision 020, sections 4.4–4.7 / table 4-11](https://www.realsenseai.com/download/21345/?tmstv=1780360410).

The **Mid-360 covers 360° azimuth and −7° to +52° elevation**. Its manual calls 0.1–0.2 m data reference-only because precision is not guaranteed, and notes unreliable detection of some small/thin/low-reflectivity targets within 1 m. An inverted mount requires at least **0.5 m** mounting-surface height. Therefore neither the advertised short range nor 360° azimuth proves precise ground coverage around this low chassis. The manual's synchronization support also does not make camera and LiDAR timestamps aligned automatically. [Manufacturer manual, printed pp. 8–9 and 20](https://terra-1-g.djicdn.com/851d20f7b9f64838a34cd02351370894/Livox/Livox_Mid-360_User_Manual_EN.pdf) ([checked local copy](../../../robot/sensors/livox_mid360/source/Livox_Mid-360_User_Manual_EN.pdf)).

Measure the following before choosing the final rig:

1. Actual rectified intrinsics, depth scale, camera/IMU/LiDAR-to-body extrinsics, clock offsets and drift, per-point LiDAR timestamps, and acquisition-to-controller latency distributions. Preserve raw XYZ/XYZW conventions explicitly; use the same body reference point for pose and twist.
2. Coverage of **all six landing sectors**, at actual optical heights and through roll, pitch, leg swing, and yaw. Include stereo occlusion, bodies, brackets, cables, and invalid depth. Compare instantaneous observations, uncertainty-limited map retention, and required footprint coverage separately.
3. Target-dependent depth error and missing returns on dark, shiny, vegetated, sloped, thin, and negative-obstacle surfaces, with independent geometric reference. A flat ideal renderer cannot provide these measurements.
4. Full added sensor/bracket/cable/compute/power mass and inertia, stiffness/vibration, thermal behavior, power transients, and onboard map/estimator latency. Reconcile these against the current asset instead of adding them to the obsolete 6.3 kg mock budget.

Use the D455 initially for forward look-ahead/replay and the Mid-360 for broader structure and local odometry. Whether lower-range side/downward cameras or another sensor are necessary should follow the measured new-footprint gaps. This is a proposed division of work, not a six-camera purchasing recommendation. With no RTK, test local odometry drift, degeneracy, relocalization, and return-to-start error; use surveyed indoor targets or another independent reference for evaluation. FAST-LIO2/LIVO2 are candidates for producing pose estimates, not proof of indefinitely accurate world coordinates. [FAST-LIO2](https://github.com/hku-mars/FAST_LIO), [FAST-LIVO2 calibration/synchronization resources](https://github.com/hku-mars/FAST-LIVO2).

## Bounded work that can proceed beside locomotion training

| Experiment | Comparison and output | What would change our choice |
|---|---|---|
| Same-terrain information ablation | Same commands, mass, torque/contact/quiet gates, and terrain seeds: blind history vs ideal geometry vs causal degraded maps. Report tracking, energy proxy, falls/contact violations, and terrain-specific successes. | If ideal perception cannot improve the feasible gait, work on control/actuation before increasing sensor complexity. |
| Acquisition/stop compatibility | Replay each candidate rig through measured motion, with missed frames, timestamp errors, uncertainty growth, and actual stop trajectories. Output whole-footprint and swept-support evidence timelines. | Whether a realizable rig and stop behavior have an overlapping operating envelope under the unchanged lease. |
| Estimator/map comparison | Recorded calibrated sensor streams: LiDAR–IMU baseline vs optional LiDAR–IMU–vision. Compare drift and map registration against independent reference, including feature-poor areas. | Add fusion complexity only when it improves a measured failure mode within onboard latency/power limits. |
| Student robustness comparison | Fixed teacher: recurrent noisy-heightmap student vs simpler history/map encoder; later CMS/RGB or direct-depth candidate if mapping errors dominate. Test blackout, partial coverage, and changed mounts. | Prefer the least complex architecture that preserves locomotion and correct uncertainty behavior on held-out cases; average reward alone is insufficient. |

The minimum reusable sensor packet should include acquisition/receipt times and clock identity; calibration identity; sensor-to-body transform; body pose plus uncertainty at acquisition; raw valid points/depth; self-mask; and provenance. The local map needs height, variance, observed/usable/unknown masks, capture age, and distinct inferred/support-eligible fields. Navigation consumes this map and demonstrated locomotion capability; locomotion receives a bounded command and appropriately versioned terrain observations. This proposed seam allows adding sensors without tying the controller to a particular brand or pretending synthetic geometry is a sensor.

## Tentative “super drone paper” match: Swift

**Kaufmann et al., Nature 2023, Champion-level drone racing using deep reinforcement learning** is a plausible match, but the user did not supply a title. Swift combines onboard visual–inertial estimation, gate detection, RL, and empirical models of perception/dynamics discrepancies collected from the real system. The lesson for us is to identify measured errors and latency before claiming sim-to-real transfer, and make the control objective account for useful sensing. Its calibration used motion-capture reference and its task used known track geometry. Those conditions do not establish general terrain navigation without RTK or external calibration. Aerial racing actions, speeds, and collision outcomes do not transfer to six-legged support control. [Paper, methods](https://rpg.ifi.uzh.ch/docs/Nature23_Kaufmann.pdf).

The review favors starting terrain information and sensor data work now, while the locomotion learner progresses independently. It does not choose a new production architecture, change any admission gate, claim hardware traversal, or infer useful walking speed from video playback.
