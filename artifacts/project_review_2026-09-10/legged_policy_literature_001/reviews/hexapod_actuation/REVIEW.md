# Hexapod speed, actuator limits and gait authority

The strongest conclusion is that the current controller is deliberately slow: its fixed swing duration, required support count and very small residual action restrict what PPO can discover. These are useful admission choices for a first reference, but they are not established physical necessities. Keep the current 1.6 N·m operating budget while comparing a new controller with authority over timing and load transfer. Published hexapod work supports learned gait parameters, direct joint targets and constrained whole-body control; it does not establish a faster safe speed for this exact robot. [Berkeley hexapod project](https://sites.google.com/view/hexapod-rl/home); [Qiu et al.](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/actuators/actuators-12-00075/article_deploy/actuators-12-00075.pdf); [Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05115v1).

This review covers eleven primary works and two manufacturer sources. Eight papers concern learned hexapod locomotion or planning, two provide complementary hexapod mechanics/optimization evidence, and one addresses morphology/control co-design on a quadruped. Abstract-only and project-page access are marked in the source matrix. No implementation from a paper was executed, and no simulation, firmware or CAD setting was changed.

## Which limits are physical?

| Current setting | What it actually constrains | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| 1.6 N·m requested torque | The present motor operating budget | Retain. Hardware-informed, but not a complete torque–speed–temperature envelope. |
| 0.04 rad per 20 ms | Successive executed position targets | Software choice: 2 rad/s, about 19.1 rpm. It is neither measured joint velocity nor the vendor's loaded speed limit. |
| 2 s swing plus 0.3 s contact hold | Reference timing and placement horizon | Software template. It restricts stride frequency before PPO acts. |
| At least five retained supports | The original one-leg wave proof | Conservative contract. A different support pattern requires its own force/contact/stability admission. |
| ±0.02 rad residual | Learned deviation from the reference | About ±1.15°. It cannot change which leg the generator permits to lift or retime a fixed swing. |

A target-rate limit is useful, but its value should eventually come from an identified actuator and mechanism model rather than from gait appearance.

The official RS05 English manual lists **1.6 N·m rated torque**, a **70 × 70 mm heat-sink condition**, a rated test at **100 rpm**, and **5.5 N·m peak** (PDF pages 10–11, printed 8–9). Its thermal discussion distinguishes stall from rotating operation. The official product index instead lists **1.8 N·m at 100 rpm**. This is an unresolved manufacturer-source discrepancy, not permission to raise the present cap. Confirm the purchased revision, mounting/cooling, current and duty conditions before revising the model. [RS05 manual](https://github.com/RobStride/Product_Information/blob/main/Product%20Literature/RS05/RS05User%20Manual260713.pdf); [manufacturer index](https://github.com/RobStride/Product_Information).

The manual's 480 rpm no-load figure is much larger than our target-rate equivalent. That numerical contrast only identifies a software choice; it does not show that a loaded joint, tibia linkage or motor thermal installation can safely follow a much faster target. No peak-duration allowance has been inferred from the overload graph.

A larger residual is also not free authority. With the current Kp = 30, an additional 0.02 rad position error contributes 0.6 N·m to the position term if every other state is held fixed. This arithmetic is not a prediction of the coupled robot's torque, but it explains why simply widening residual amplitude can consume substantial margin. The useful change is to give the policy better ways to manage timing, support and body/foot placement while preserving physical limits.

The strongest architecture alternatives for this project are H05's explicit prior/no-prior contrast, H06's learned duty and phase, H07's force allocation, H08's separation of physical constraints from motion objectives, and H09's motion-style guidance. A soft motion prior can encourage insect-like coordination without making every target a fixed replay. This is an architectural distinction; it does not guarantee better reward or transfer on the C study. [H05](https://sites.google.com/view/hexapod-rl/home); [H06](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/actuators/actuators-12-00075/article_deploy/actuators-12-00075.pdf); [H07](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s10033-024-01000-0); [H08](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05115v1); [H09](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03167v1).

The literature is not unanimous evidence for one controller. Low-dimensional priors improve structure and can reduce the learning problem, while also excluding behaviors outside their family. Decentralization introduces another plausible bias, with simulation-only evidence in H02. More powerful torque-controlled hexapods and compliant RHex mechanisms cannot supply numerical speed or torque promises for this robot. Training algorithm names alone therefore do not resolve the bottleneck.

## Support, speed and efficiency

A support count is not a force-feasibility certificate. As a mechanics inference, even a large projected COM polygon can coexist with an unloaded foot, excessive joint torque or insufficient friction. Conversely, a three-contact pattern may be statically feasible when the contacts and COM satisfy the appropriate geometry and force constraints. Dynamic support requires acceleration and centroidal-force analysis in addition to a static polygon. H04 and H07 explicitly motivate transition/force checks; H10 demonstrates why five contacts are not a universal hexapod requirement. [Transition feasibility](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0328.pdf); [force allocation](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s10033-024-01000-0); [RHex mechanics](https://kodlab.seas.upenn.edu/uploads/Kod/Saranli00.pdf).

For the present template, nominal one-leg scheduling takes 6 × (2 + 0.3) = **13.8 s per cycle**. Three paired transfers take 3 × (2 + 0.3) = **6.9 s**. These are reference-clock calculations, excluding reconciliation and stopping; they are not minimum physical cycle times. A long placement horizon also demands a long stance excursion for a given body speed. Faster locomotion may therefore require changing support overlap and placement horizon rather than stretching the existing trajectory further.

The local evidence already separates these effects:

- The [single-leg CPU study](/Users/andreboufama/Documents/CUPI/HEXAPOD/tmp/reference_speed_feasibility_001/report.json) rejected all twelve faster duration/speed combinations on the existing target-derivative budgets. This shows incompatibility with that reference, not physical impossibility.
- The [planned paired-target study](/Users/andreboufama/Documents/CUPI/HEXAPOD/tmp/reference_pair_speed_feasibility_001/CANDIDATES.json) found 0.02 m/s candidates at 2 s and 1.5 s swings within its target-only checks. The separate [contact-aware ideal fixture](/Users/andreboufama/Documents/CUPI/HEXAPOD/tmp/reference_pair_motion_001/report_002.json) still rejected its 0.015/0.02 m/s cases on retained support. A feasible target sequence is not contact admission.
- Actual [paired001](/Users/andreboufama/Documents/CUPI/HEXAPOD/tmp/reference_pair_motion_results_001/actual_review/report.json) executed the full 48 s screen, including 24 s at a 0.01 m/s request, with 22 qualified landings across all six feet, at least four supports and a 400 Hz post-startup requested-torque peak of 1.3970 N·m. It remained **rejected**: the original 50 Hz position/integrated-rate discrepancy was 5.5798 mm against a 5 mm gate. The independent 400 Hz discrepancy was about 6.9793 mm. Initial reset requested torque reached 63.66 N·m before the applied clamp; this was excluded from settled scoring and is not a qualified hardware startup.

The residual-policy learning campaign is outside this evidence snapshot. These figures describe a reference controller, not a learned all-direction policy. The unresolved rate/position discrepancy must remain visible; selecting whichever metric produces a pass would not establish reliable dynamics. [Independent rate-gap review](/Users/andreboufama/Documents/CUPI/HEXAPOD/tmp/reference_pair_root_gap_review_001/README.md).

Slow motion is not automatically energy-efficient. Our inference is that prolonged supporting torque can accumulate heating while achieving little distance. Future comparisons should report bus electrical energy per metre, actuator temperature/current duty and task success, alongside positive mechanical work and torque percentiles. A visually smooth gait or a low instantaneous peak alone cannot establish efficiency. The hardware measurements needed to distinguish these quantities have not been supplied.

## Morphology and the femur/tibia decision

The co-design result in H11 supports comparing geometry and controller together; it does not identify a particular leg ratio. For this project, the mechanics inference is that joint load depends on the contact force and its moment arm, approximately through Jᵀf plus gravity and inertial terms. A longer tibia can reduce a particular joint's torque if it changes stance orientation and load distribution favorably. It can also increase inertia or an unfavorable lever arm. Total length alone does not determine the sign. [Mechanical/control co-design](https://la.disneyresearch.com/publication/concurrent-optimization-of-mechanical-design-and-locomotion-control-of-a-legged-robot/); [hexapod dynamics formulation](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurorobotics/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2021.627157/full).

Shorter femurs can help a fixed load case, but can reduce stride workspace and require faster joint motion to achieve the same foot speed. A lower body changes horizontal lever arms, knee posture, clearance and collision exposure simultaneously. A higher stance is not necessarily worse, nor a crouched stance necessarily efficient. Compare body height, foot spread, duty factor and control policy jointly over forward, reverse, strafe, turn, arcs and terrain; preserve motor packaging, hard stops and realistic contact pads.

The selected C study is a screening morphology. Production CAD geometry, linkage transmission, masses, centres of mass and inertias must be regenerated together before a final manufacturing decision. Borrowed inertias and a serial approximation cannot establish the optimum of the production four-bar mechanism. Keep coxa fixed as requested and reject designs that do not fit the motors, regardless of a simulated reward advantage. [Model conventions and production constraints](/Users/andreboufama/Documents/CUPI/HEXAPOD/AGENTS.md).

## Proposed next comparison

Run a small, explicitly versioned architecture comparison rather than spending all available training on ever-smaller residual corrections to this clock. This is a proposed experiment, not an adopted policy or gate change.

1. Keep the frozen reference-residual branch as the controlled baseline. Retain its historical support and numerical gates exactly.
2. Add a branch with authority over phase/cadence, duty, foothold displacement and modest body/stance adjustment. Train or optimize load transfer using measured contact and a torque/friction feasibility calculation. Keep both-foot landing qualification for paired motion and define the observation state explicitly; the old scalar 846/849 packet is not a paired-controller contract.
3. Include a direct stance-relative joint-target PPO baseline with the same asset, command distribution, actuator budget and sampling budget. Use identified actuation and action regularization; do not impose the old five-support clock on a branch intended to learn its own coordination. Define its physical acceptance criteria before training, with no retrospective relabeling of old failures.
4. Compare speed tracking, all-direction stability, stop distance/latency, contact slip, actual target/torque limits and energy/thermal duty on held-out runs. Retain position- and reported-rate measurements independently until their discrepancy has a defensible resolution. Gate terrain/perception progression on the actual controller envelope, not on a reference animation.

The near-term practical choice is **more useful policy authority and explicit load allocation before more motor torque**. The final choice between a flexible reference and direct targets should be made by matched C-study experiments. No reviewed paper makes the present 2 s, five-support or ±0.02 rad settings mandatory, and none removes the need to validate this robot's actuator, contact and sensing interfaces.
