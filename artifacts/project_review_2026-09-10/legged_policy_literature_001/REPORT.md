# Legged policy training for the survey hexapod

The most useful next experiment is to recover the earlier omnidirectional PPO's capability while directly training its missing behaviors: quiet standing, smooth starts and stops, and smooth motion across translation and turning commands. A second controller family should allow learned changes to foot placement and gait timing. The current fixed, slow reference with a small joint residual is useful for physical diagnostics, but its action space gives PPO little opportunity to discover a substantially different gait.

This recommendation combines the project's measured failures with a breadth-first comparison of 32 distinct locomotion, mapping and experimental-design works. It is an engineering judgment, not a published demonstration on this robot. The evidence does not establish that direct joint control, a learned gait generator, or a whole-body controller will win under this assembly's mass, geometry and motor limits. The fastest defensible route is a small, matched comparison that can reject weak candidates early.

The mission is autonomous coverage of an operator-bounded area without RTK while carrying a steady data-collection deck. Stage 2 supplies reliable body motion; Stage 3 adds terrain capability. Sensor-aware navigation, hardware dynamics and the production four-bar assembly require their own qualification. An attractive gait is useful evidence of quality, but speed, stability, actuator demand, stopping, sensing and reproducibility determine whether it supports the mission.

## Evidence and scope

The review spans direct position policies, gait-generator modulation, motion priors, decentralized hexapod control, force allocation, energy-based gait emergence, adaptation, perceptive locomotion and navigation. The three detailed reviews contain 35 entries with four overlaps; the additional 2026 observation-selection paper brings the distinct core inventory to 32. Swift drone racing is a separate, tentative cross-domain match for the earlier “super drone” reference. [Sources and reading limits](SOURCES.json) identify primary URLs and the exact review responsible for each entry.

The source base includes real quadrupeds, simulated and real hexapods, and model-based methods as well as reinforcement learning. Hardware demonstrations on another robot are stronger than an abstract alone, but neither proves transfer to the C study. Some full methods were inaccessible: the Berkeley hexapod thesis link returned 404, and two recent publisher/PDF endpoints rejected access. Those gaps are retained explicitly; abstracts and search excerpts do not support detailed implementation claims.

## What the local results actually establish

The C-study robot has six legs and 18 joints, 72.5 mm femurs, 126 mm tibias, fixed coxa geometry and 8.26081134 kg of transferred CAD mass/inertia. The applied motor cap is 1.6 N·m. These are the study's controlled conditions, not certification of the physical four-bar robot. [Study contract](../../../experiments/c_length_study/README.md).

The preserved 315-actor/318-critic omnidirectional checkpoint demonstrated multi-direction motion. Two short fine-tunes improved some tracking but did not resolve standing oscillation. That result rejects those tested interventions; it does not establish that direct PPO is inherently unable to stand still. The baseline checkpoint must remain recoverable and receive the same cold evaluation as successors. [Earlier policy preview](../../omni_flat_2026-09-09/omni_preview_001/rollout.mp4), [training-recipe review](reviews/training_recipes/REVIEW.md).

The newer 846/849 reference-residual interface has evidence of quiet controlled stepping, but it has not recovered the earlier policy's complete omnidirectional capability. Its displayed diagnostic requests 0.005 m/s, with roughly a 13.8-second six-leg cycle. The slow speed comes from the command and stepping template, not video playback. The fixed two-second swing, five retained supports and ±0.02 rad residual are chosen software restrictions. More PPO updates do not automatically grant control over the missing gait variables. [Recorded diagnostic](../../omni_diagnostics_2026-09-09/reference_progress_recording_001/README.md).

Static four-support transfers and the paired-motion experiment justify investigating a less restrictive contact schedule. They do not establish a successful faster policy: paired motion retained useful torque/support evidence but failed the unchanged displacement-versus-integrated-velocity gate. The native rate discrepancy remains unresolved and must stay visible alongside independent pose/angle measurements. [Paired evidence](../../omni_diagnostics_2026-09-09/reference_pair_motion_001/README.md), [independent discrepancy review](../../omni_diagnostics_2026-09-09/reference_pair_root_gap_review_001/README.md).

## The next architecture comparison

First, cold-evaluate the preserved omnidirectional checkpoint in its explicitly identified physical configuration. The historical repair diagnostic used a 0.03 rad target-step limit, solver 16/4 and external-force timing disabled by default. Reference009 used 0.04 rad, 16/1 and external-force timing enabled. These are comparison confounds. A new formal 0.04 evaluation must be named and recorded as such; changing its physics while attributing a difference entirely to policy learning would be misleading.

Then compare a command-balanced curriculum alone with that same curriculum plus separately logged policy smoothness regularization. Include sustained zero command, starts from quiet stance, moving-to-zero transitions, low-speed motion, pure yaw, reversals and combined commands. Preserve coverage of every direction so a policy cannot improve its average by abandoning difficult bearings. A short allocation should earn continuation through measured behavior, not training reward alone.

CAPS motivates smoothing the deterministic policy mapping rather than relying exclusively on an action-change reward. For this implementation, temporal pairs must not cross episode resets, and command changes require deliberate handling so the loss does not teach delayed stopping or delayed turning. Spatial perturbations should represent plausible sensor variation in explicit units; blindly adding Gaussian noise to contact flags, clocks and command categories is not a faithful implementation. These are proposed implementation safeguards, not additional published CAPS results. [CAPS paper and project](https://ai.bu.edu/caps/).

The independent alternative should expose meaningful phase, cadence, foot placement and body-motion authority, inspired by PMTG and hexapod gait adaptation. Its output must still pass executable joint, contact and actuator checks. Widening the position residual alone is a poor substitute: with Kp=30, a 0.02 rad target change adds 0.6 N·m to the proportional term when other state is fixed. That calculation is not the total realized torque, but it shows why extra action authority needs an explicit physical test. [PMTG](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v87/iscen18a.html), [actuation review](reviews/hexapod_actuation/REVIEW.md).

| Candidate | First informative comparison | Continue when | Reject or redirect when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preserved direct PPO with focused curriculum | Cold baseline, then bounded stand/stop-balanced update | Quiet behavior improves while every commanded family retains motion | A stationary or slow solution buys reward by losing tracking |
| Same curriculum with smoothness regularization | Matched seed, data budget, physics and evaluations | Less action/body jitter without worse stop or tracking response | Regularization suppresses necessary movement or hides limiter saturation |
| Learnable phase/placement controller | Zero-output and controlled authority screens, then bounded PPO | Measured load transfer and command response justify its extra complexity | Fixed generator restrictions still dominate or actuator demand becomes infeasible |
| Fresh direct PPO | Controlled comparator if inherited behavior persists | Removes a demonstrated inherited failure under equal accounting | Lower initial capability consumes compute without a new informative trend |
| Current fixed-wave residual | Small physical/reset diagnostics | Answers a specific reliability question | More updates merely reproduce the template's speed ceiling |

A future larger comparison can use a predeclared factorial design instead of changing many ingredients at once. Freeman and Bauer's 2026 hexapod study reduced observation-combination experiments using fractional factorial designs and repeated trials. It studied DDPG with a CPG in simulation, not this PPO controller. Its useful lesson is disciplined factor selection and replication; its selected observation set is not a drop-in sensor specification. [Full primary article](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/17298806261431893).

## Physical constraints and geometry

Retain the 1.6 N·m study cap. RobStride's current repository index and its English RS05 manual disagree on rated torque: 1.8 versus 1.6 N·m. The manual's continuous specification also includes a heat-sink condition; its peak value is not an indefinite operating allowance. Exact purchased revision, supply voltage, thermal installation and measured torque-speed-current behavior must resolve this before expanding a physical operating envelope. [Official index](https://github.com/RobStride/Product_Information), [RS05 manual](https://github.com/RobStride/Product_Information/blob/main/Product%20Literature/RS05/RS05User%20Manual260713.pdf).

Leg length is not independently optimal: joint torque depends on posture, the direction of ground reaction forces, their distribution across supporting feet, link gravity and acceleration. A longer tibia can reduce a particular joint's peak in one posture while worsening reach, inertia or another joint's load. Morphology and control therefore need matched comparisons, including assembly feasibility and motor packaging. The current C selection remains the execution priority; neither this literature review nor one gait screen proves its global hardware optimum. [Mechanical/control co-design precedent](https://la.disneyresearch.com/publication/concurrent-optimization-of-mechanical-design-and-locomotion-control-of-a-legged-robot/), [Smart Gait](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s10033-024-01000-0).

Energy should be a measured outcome as well as a reward candidate. Report progress, elapsed simulated time, mechanical work or the precisely defined proxy, and motor demand together. A robot that barely moves can score low energy while failing the mission. Cost per distance is poorly conditioned near zero speed, so quiet standing and useful translation need separate reporting. Published energy-driven gait emergence motivates experimentation, not assuming that an insect-like appearance identifies the most efficient solution. [Fu et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.01674).

## Terrain and perception in parallel

Keep three parallel development conditions: proprioception alone, privileged terrain geometry, and causal noisy sensor-derived terrain. Use identical terrain/direction cases for the comparison. If privileged geometry cannot produce reliable traversal, perception is not the immediate bottleneck. If privileged control works but sensor-driven control fails, separate coverage, registration, data age and student inference. Mild blind walking can remain useful during degraded sensing without being mistaken for the ability to select an unseen foothold. [Lee](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11251), [Miki](https://leggedrobotics.github.io/rl-perceptiveloco/), [CMS](https://antonilo.github.io/vision_locomotion/).

Miki's privileged teacher and recurrent student provide a strong initial pattern for noisy local terrain inputs. RMA and DreamWaQ add relevant proprioceptive history/adaptation ideas. CMS offers another route if visual simulation becomes the bottleneck, but requires useful real walking data for cross-modal supervision. Extreme Parkour is valuable for later terrain curricula and depth learning; obstacle-conditioned progression is a different objective from arbitrary continuous planar twist tracking.

The owned Mid-360 and D455 are starting points, not purchasing constraints or approved mounts. Evaluate proposed sensor poses against the complete robot, minimum useful range, self-occlusion and the terrain footprint required throughout a step or stop. Mid-360 orientation restrictions and D455 mode-dependent near-range limits matter at this robot's scale. The detailed review links the manufacturer specifications and distinguishes hypothetical six-camera coverage from measured hardware. [Sensor and terrain review](reviews/terrain_perception/REVIEW.md).

A sensor packet should carry acquisition and receipt time, clock and calibration identity, sensor-to-body transform, acquisition-time pose uncertainty, valid observations and self-masking. A local terrain representation should distinguish measured height, variance, age, unknown cells, inferred geometry and support eligibility. A pit floor can be accurately mapped while being unusable for the intended step. Local elevation mapping is appropriate for ordinary stepping; overhangs and body clearance require additional three-dimensional information. [Probabilistic elevation mapping](https://doi.org/10.1109/LRA.2018.2849506), [GPU elevation mapping](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12876), [ANYmal Parkour](https://sites.google.com/leggedrobotics.com/agile-navigation).

The current 250 ms map-evidence lease and the slow reference's measured 5.54-second stop do not yet form a demonstrated operating envelope. Resolve the mismatch through faster validated stopping, actual reacquisition, registration uncertainty and explicit planted-support evidence. Extending the timeout alone does not establish safety. This is a measured integration problem that terrain and sensing work should address together. [Stop evidence](../../omni_diagnostics_2026-09-09/reference_physics_009/README.md), [support-envelope prototype](../../perception_readiness_2026-09-09/support_supervisor_001/README.md).

For navigation, retain a body-frame command of forward velocity, leftward velocity and yaw rate for continuous walking. Their simultaneous values naturally describe translation while turning, including arcs and sideways motion; a gait does not need a separate actor for each path shape. The planner should respect measured capability and stopping behavior. VP-Nav is a relevant planning/control precedent. For later climbing or constrained maneuvers, ANYmal Parkour shows why local position, heading and allotted time may become useful skill commands. [VP-Nav](https://navigation-locomotion.github.io/), [ANYmal Parkour](https://sites.google.com/leggedrobotics.com/agile-navigation).

FAST-LIO2 is a reasonable Mid-360 local-estimation baseline; FAST-LIVO2 is a candidate if adding synchronized vision improves an observed weakness. Neither establishes mission-wide drift bounds without testing. Swift's drone-racing result adds a useful lesson about measuring real dynamics, perception error and latency, but its known track and calibration conditions are different from bounded-area terrain surveying. The Swift identification remains tentative. [FAST-LIO2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.06829), [FAST-LIVO2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14035), [Swift methods](https://rpg.ifi.uzh.ch/docs/Nature23_Kaufmann.pdf).

## Evidence that earns more compute

Use common cold evaluations, checkpoint hashes, exact source/asset/configuration identities and the unchanged 0.040 rad per 20 ms formal playback limit. Keep SDK rates and independent interval-angle measurements separate. Report each direction, both yaw signs, combined commands, path changes, starts/stops and sustained quiet; averages can conceal a missing capability. Record at real simulated speed with requested and achieved commands visible, including ground arrows for path demonstrations.

For short learning comparisons, measure valid transitions, resets, wall time, simulation time and optimizer updates separately. Retain intermediate checkpoints as explicitly unqualified recovery artifacts when possible, so a late failure does not erase the ability to inspect earlier learning. A checkpoint is not an admitted policy until its cold evaluations finish. Increase environment count from measured integrated throughput and memory, not another paper's hardware timing.

Published training times range from minutes to many hours and often cover only one stage. Rudin's fast results used an RTX A6000, while other reviewed methods used different robots, simulators, GPUs and success criteria. These are feasibility precedents rather than Spark ETAs. The comparison should track simulated experience and real resource cost, then expand only candidates with a useful measured trend. [Training-cost inventory](reviews/training_recipes/source_matrix.json).

Stage 2 remains the full quantitative and visual gate, with all motion families matching the accepted forward quality. Stage 3 requires the final frozen terrain envelope, untouched qualification layouts, retained flat behavior, actuator/contact limits and sensor-map validity where sensing is claimed. Neither an entry-standing test nor a literature-backed architecture closes either stage. The detailed source matrices and three specialist reviews support future decisions without rewriting any historical result.
