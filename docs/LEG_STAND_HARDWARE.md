# Single-leg stand hardware recommendation — 8 September 2026

**Use direct sensors for force and carriage position, with vision as an independent view of the linkage.** The confirmed rig lets the body/coxa mounting carriage move vertically while restricting its other motion. A rail encoder measures that one translation; it does not measure the complete leg pose. Record all three motor encoders too, and observe the passive linkage independently when checking backlash and flex.

This is a researched component recommendation, not a purchased or commissioned system. The user requires stocked parts with public prices and direct checkout, without custom quotes. The current procurement plan assumes delivery to Cornell in the US. Final load range and scale length depend on the carriage/ballast mass, stroke and intended impacts. Simulation/training remain paused pending the revised leg export.

## Recommended build

| Measurement | Preferred hardware | Installation / purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vertical carriage position | Phidgets **ENC4110_0**, 300 mm linear encoder, with **ENC1001_0 + HUB0002_1** readout | Choose this length only if it covers the actual carriage stroke with end clearance. Mount the scale on the frame and readhead on the carriage; this measuring scale does not replace the load-bearing rail. Establish a unique home datum because index marks repeat. |
| Vertical foot force | VPG/Tedea-Huntleigh **00042-020K-G3-00X**, 20 kg (about **196 N**), with **PhidgetBridge 1046_1** | Four-wire bridge cell below a stiff, replaceable ground-contact plate. Suitable starting candidate for static and slow tests; qualify plate response and loading before repeated stepping or impact identification. |
| Joint motion | Existing RS05 encoder/velocity feedback and logged command targets | Keep raw timestamps and controller mode/gains. Motor-side feedback alone cannot independently establish passive-knee motion or structural deflection. Add a passive-joint encoder if that is the quantity being identified. |
| Full linkage motion and deformation | Calibrated **global-shutter camera**, initially targeting **120–240 fps**; a second view when needed for occlusion or out-of-plane motion | Track rigid markers on carriage, femur, tibia, lever and rod. Use fixed focus/exposure and sufficient lighting. Calibrate spatial scale and lens distortion. |
| Electrical and thermal response | Bus voltage/current logging, motor feedback and motor/mount temperature sensors | Separate commanded torque, reported torque/current and independently measured foot force. Record the actual supply and heat path. |

The ENC4110 lists 5 µm/pulse resolution, 300 mm travel and 1 m/s maximum speed. Installed alignment, reference handling and mounting still determine position accuracy. An initial installed-position error target of **0.1 mm or better** is an engineering target to verify against an independent reference, not a product guarantee. The ENC1001 supports a 1 ms data interval with a 1 Mbit/s VINT port, which the HUB0002 provides. Verify the scale's count-to-distance conversion experimentally rather than applying quadrature multiplication twice. [Encoder](https://www.phidgets.com/?prodid=1106), [counter interface](https://www.phidgets.com/?prodid=1332), [USB-C hub](https://www.phidgets.com/?prodid=1361).

The VPG 0042 family specifies a maximum **400 × 400 mm platform**, 2 mV/V nominal output, 5–15 V excitation, four-wire cable as standard, and **150% maximum safe central overload**. That overload figure is not the normal measuring range or an eccentric-impact rating. Follow the manufacturer's mounting drawing and use overload stops that remain clear during valid measurements. The weighing specifications do not establish the frequency response of the assembled foot plate or its fatigue life under repeated impacts. [VPG 0042 datasheet, February 2025](https://vlite.vpgforcesensors.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Model-0042-WS-DS_g.pdf).

For orientation, the current modeled robot's ideal equal three-foot static share is approximately **8.26 × 9.81 / 3 = 27 N per foot**. The user expects less than **10 kg-equivalent (about 98 N)** on one leg; this is a provisional design expectation, not a measured peak. The earlier 50 kg recommendation lacked a load case justifying that range and is superseded by the 20 kg candidate. That provides nominal headroom for plate tare and uncertain peaks without sizing around an unsupported 490 N requirement. It is not a demonstrated dynamic safety factor.

A **10 kg cell** may suit a lighter, controlled test if the total applied load, including plate tare, remains comfortably below its capacity and the cyclic/eccentric loading stays within its separate specifications. Merely expecting peaks below 10 kg is insufficient to choose a 10 kg cell. Taring the plate does not restore mechanical capacity. Final sizing must use the actual moving mass, ballast, acceleration and intended foot contacts; validate impact tests separately. The roughly 1.1 kN internal four-bar rod-load estimate is a different force and cannot be used directly as the required ground-load-cell range.

## Acquisition hardware

**If already available in the lab:** a multi-slot NI CompactDAQ chassis with an **NI-9237 bridge-input module** and **NI-9401 counter-access module** is an option for acquisition using a shared hardware clock. The NI-9237 supplies bridge conditioning/excitation and simultaneous sampling up to 50 kS/s/channel. NI documents the 9401 as a supported way to access chassis counters for encoder measurements. Confirm the exact chassis, DAQmx host/OS support and clock routing; a differential encoder output needs an appropriate receiver before a 5 V TTL input. Use a lab PC for this if convenient—the stand does not have to depend on the Jetson. Do not delay the first measurements waiting for an NI quote. [NI-9237](https://www.ni.com/en/shop/hardware/strain--pressure--and-force/model-ni-9237), [NI counter-module support](https://www.ni.com/en/support/documentation/supplemental/18/cdaq-module-support-for-accessing-on-board-counters.html).

**Recommended first purchase:** the **PhidgetBridge 1046_1** can read one force channel at up to 1,200 samples/s. With two, three or four active inputs its published maxima fall to 250, 167 and 125 samples/s respectively. Use its DataRate API for sub-millisecond operation and verify achieved timing on the actual device. Its nominal 5 V bridge connection and ratiometric input suit the selected cell; check excitation under load, wire from the cell's own pinout, and calibrate the complete chain at a fixed gain. The counter and force bridge do **not** automatically share an acquisition clock. This kit is useful for initial measurements, but timing/latency must be established before fitting dynamic phase response. [PhidgetBridge specifications and integration guide](https://www.phidgets.com/?prodid=1270).

For the standard VPG four-wire cable, the manufacturer identifies green as positive excitation, black as negative excitation, red as positive signal and white as negative signal. Connect those respectively to bridge 5V, G, + and −; confirm against the supplied cell documentation because other brands use different colors. Verify excitation at the connected cell is at least 5 V: that is the VPG's specified lower limit, while the bridge board itself accepts USB supply down to 4.5 V. Do not connect an external 10 V supply to the board's 5 V terminal. [VPG wiring and excitation specification](https://vlite.vpgforcesensors.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Model-0042-WS-DS_g.pdf), [bridge electrical limits](https://www.phidgets.com/?prodid=1270).

A custom microcontroller logger is another option if the electrical team already has the expertise: hardware encoder counters, a properly conditioned bridge ADC, a common hardware timebase and buffered output to the PC. It is extra development and calibration work, not necessary before useful measurements. A bare load cell cannot connect directly to a general GPIO input.

I would not choose an **HX711** as the primary dynamics instrument. Its standard output is 10 or 80 samples/s, useful for weighing and slow sweeps but restrictive for transient identification. [SparkFun HX711 board](https://www.sparkfun.com/sparkfun-load-cell-amplifier-hx711.html).

## Timing, vision and fixture requirements

Design for synchronized force and encoder records at roughly **1–2 kHz** for the initial identification work. This is a proposed acquisition target; it does not mean a 1 kHz mechanical measurement bandwidth. Acquire at a supported native DAQ rate, retain raw data, and document filtering/decimation and group delay. Faster contact-impact work may require higher acquisition rates and a separately characterized plate/cell response. Timestamp samples at acquisition; USB callback arrival time alone is inadequate for phase-sensitive fitting. Preserve CAN reception times and characterize motor-feedback delay rather than inventing motor sampling times.

A normal camera is useful for slow geometric checks and visual inspection. For dynamic measurements, global shutter avoids the row-by-row timing distortion of rolling shutter, but exposure blur, calibration error and occlusion still remain. Use a common trigger or independently verified timing marker. A single calibrated side view can measure a known planar motion; it cannot qualify arbitrary three-dimensional leg motion. Camera-derived acceleration also needs careful filtering because differentiation amplifies position noise. [Basler shutter documentation](https://docs.baslerweb.com/electronic-shutter-types).

Use a stiff frame and well-spaced rail carriages with an adjustable body/coxa fixture, carriage catch/end stops and a replaceable foot-contact plate. Measure moving mass and rail friction. Avoid any plate contact, stiff cable or guide friction that bypasses the measured vertical load path. Calibrate with known loads at the center and several intended foot positions, in increasing and decreasing load order; test repeatability, motor-on electrical noise and timing with an independent reference. A single-axis cell measures normal force only: add measured horizontal force or a multi-axis transducer when identifying friction/shear. The constrained stand cannot establish free-body balance or lateral/torsional strength by itself.
