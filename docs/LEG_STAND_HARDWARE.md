# Single-leg stand hardware recommendation — 8 September 2026

**Use direct sensors for force and carriage position, with vision as an independent view of the linkage.** The confirmed rig lets the body/coxa mounting carriage move vertically while restricting its other motion. A rail encoder measures that one translation; it does not measure the complete leg pose. Record all three motor encoders too, and observe the passive linkage independently when checking backlash and flex.

This is a researched component recommendation, not a purchased or commissioned system. Final load range, scale length and acquisition hardware depend on the carriage/ballast mass, stroke, intended impacts, available lab equipment and budget. Simulation/training remain paused pending the revised leg export.

## Recommended build

| Measurement | Preferred hardware | Installation / purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vertical carriage position | RLS **LM10 incremental magnetic readhead + compatible MS scale**, selecting approximately 5–10 µm output resolution | Scale on the fixed frame and head on the carriage, or the reverse. Include a reference/home datum; match output electrical levels and counter bandwidth. Choose scale length from the actual stroke. |
| Vertical foot force | HBK/HBM **PW10A 50 kg**, order **1-PW10AC3/50KG-1**, provisionally about 490 N capacity | A properly mounted single-point cell below a stiff, replaceable ground-contact plate. It is intended for platform loading with off-center compensation; final capacity must include plate tare and dynamic loads. |
| Joint motion | Existing RS05 encoder/velocity feedback and logged command targets | Keep raw timestamps and controller mode/gains. Motor-side feedback alone cannot independently establish passive-knee motion or structural deflection. Add a passive-joint encoder if that is the quantity being identified. |
| Full linkage motion and deformation | Calibrated **global-shutter camera**, initially targeting **120–240 fps**; a second view when needed for occlusion or out-of-plane motion | Track rigid markers on carriage, femur, tibia, lever and rod. Use fixed focus/exposure and sufficient lighting. Calibrate spatial scale and lens distortion. |
| Electrical and thermal response | Bus voltage/current logging, motor feedback and motor/mount temperature sensors | Separate commanded torque, reported torque/current and independently measured foot force. Record the actual supply and heat path. |

The LM10 is contactless and offers selectable incremental outputs; its scale grade, alignment, readhead gap, thermal effects and reference handling still determine installed accuracy. Resolution is not accuracy. An initial installed-position error target of **0.1 mm or better** is my engineering target to verify against an independent reference, not a product guarantee. Keep the scale away from strong motor fields and test it with the motors powered. [RLS LM10](https://www.rls.si/eng/lm10-linear-and-rotary-magnetic-encoder-system), [compatible MS scales](https://www.rls.si/eng/ms-scale?openConfigurator=true).

The PW10A datasheet allows a maximum 600 × 500 mm platform and specifies a 150% limit load at no more than 100 mm eccentricity. Its 300% breaking-load figure is not a safe working-load rating. Follow the specified mounting ends, spacers and cable-side clearance. External overload stops must avoid touching during valid measurements. Its platform rating does not establish accurate measurement of arbitrary shear loads or impact bandwidth. [HBK datasheet, September 2024](https://www.hbm.com/fileadmin/mediapool/hbmdoc/technical/B02190.pdf), [mounting instructions](https://www.hbm.com/fileadmin/mediapool/hbmdoc/technical/A02323.pdf).

For orientation, the current modeled robot's ideal equal three-foot static share is approximately **8.26 × 9.81 / 3 = 27 N per foot**. That is not the maximum force on the test stand. Size the cell using the actual loaded rig and planned tests. The roughly 1.1 kN internal four-bar rod-load estimate is a different force and cannot be used directly as the required ground-load-cell range.

## Acquisition hardware

**Preferred lab setup:** an available multi-slot NI CompactDAQ chassis with an **NI-9237 bridge-input module** and **NI-9401 counter-access module**. The NI-9237 supplies bridge conditioning/excitation and simultaneous sampling up to 50 kS/s/channel. NI documents the 9401 as a supported way to access chassis counters for encoder measurements. Confirm the exact chassis, DAQmx host/OS support and clock routing before ordering; a differential encoder output needs an appropriate receiver before a 5 V TTL input. Use a lab PC for this if convenient—the stand does not have to depend on the Jetson. [NI-9237](https://www.ni.com/en/shop/hardware/strain--pressure--and-force/model-ni-9237), [NI counter-module support](https://www.ni.com/en/support/documentation/supplemental/18/cdaq-module-support-for-accessing-on-board-counters.html).

**Lower-cost first measurements:** the **PhidgetBridge 1046_1** can read one force channel at up to 1,200 samples/s. With two, three or four active inputs its published maxima fall to 250, 167 and 125 samples/s respectively. An independently connected encoder does not automatically share its acquisition clock. This is useful for initial force measurements, but timing/latency must be established before fitting dynamic phase response. Choose the six-wire cell connection and excitation/sense arrangement according to the interface documentation. [PhidgetBridge specifications and integration guide](https://www.phidgets.com/?prodid=1270).

A custom microcontroller logger is another option if the electrical team already has the expertise: hardware encoder counters, a properly conditioned bridge ADC, a common hardware timebase and buffered output to the PC. It is extra development and calibration work, not necessary before useful measurements. A bare load cell cannot connect directly to a general GPIO input.

I would not choose an **HX711** as the primary dynamics instrument. Its standard output is 10 or 80 samples/s, useful for weighing and slow sweeps but restrictive for transient identification. [SparkFun HX711 board](https://www.sparkfun.com/sparkfun-load-cell-amplifier-hx711.html).

## Timing, vision and fixture requirements

Design for synchronized force and encoder records at roughly **1–2 kHz** for the initial identification work. This is a proposed acquisition target; it does not mean a 1 kHz mechanical measurement bandwidth. Acquire at a supported native DAQ rate, retain raw data, and document filtering/decimation and group delay. Faster contact-impact work may require higher acquisition rates and a separately characterized plate/cell response. Timestamp samples at acquisition; USB callback arrival time alone is inadequate for phase-sensitive fitting. Preserve CAN reception times and characterize motor-feedback delay rather than inventing motor sampling times.

A normal camera is useful for slow geometric checks and visual inspection. For dynamic measurements, global shutter avoids the row-by-row timing distortion of rolling shutter, but exposure blur, calibration error and occlusion still remain. Use a common trigger or independently verified timing marker. A single calibrated side view can measure a known planar motion; it cannot qualify arbitrary three-dimensional leg motion. Camera-derived acceleration also needs careful filtering because differentiation amplifies position noise. [Basler shutter documentation](https://docs.baslerweb.com/electronic-shutter-types).

Use a stiff frame and well-spaced rail carriages with an adjustable body/coxa fixture, carriage catch/end stops and a replaceable foot-contact plate. Measure moving mass and rail friction. Avoid any plate contact, stiff cable or guide friction that bypasses the measured vertical load path. Calibrate with known loads at the center and several intended foot positions, in increasing and decreasing load order; test repeatability, motor-on electrical noise and timing with an independent reference. A single-axis cell measures normal force only: add measured horizontal force or a multi-axis transducer when identifying friction/shear. The constrained stand cannot establish free-body balance or lateral/torsional strength by itself.

The existing [leg-stand test sequence](../artifacts/project_review_2026-09-04/LEG_TEST_STAND.md) and [CAD-engineer handoff](CAD_ENGINEER_HANDOFF.md) specify how these measurements update a versioned dynamics model. No sensor or camera purchase alone makes the simulation a measured 1:1 model.
